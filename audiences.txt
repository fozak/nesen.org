Good document. Now let me define each audience precisely for content writing:

1. Entrepreneur
pain:     cold outreach is exhausting and doesn't convert
outcome:  steady warm referral flow that replaces cold entirely
trigger:  just lost a deal that came from cold, or closed one from a referral
proof:    classification exclusivity — no competitor in the room
rule:     speak to the referral ROI, not the networking experience
CTA:      /for-founders or /get-referrals
2. Startup Founder
pain:     building in isolation, no feedback, no warm intros to investors
outcome:  validated concept + named introductions to investors/customers
trigger:  pitch just got rejected, or idea feels stuck
proof:    Samantha → XYZ → $2M
rule:     speak to speed of progress, not community feeling
CTA:      /deliver-winning-pitch or /for-founders
3. Executive
pain:     no external network outside org chart, blind to what's coming
outcome:  early visibility into innovations + personal network on own terms
trigger:  startup just disrupted their industry and they missed it
proof:    weekly pitch segment = private scouting session
rule:     speak to independence and information advantage, not networking
CTA:      /for-executives
4. Scientist / Researcher
pain:     deep expertise, no path from lab to market
outcome:  co-founder + first customer conversation + commercial clarity
trigger:  grant ended, or colleague just commercialized similar work
proof:    "the room compresses years of trial into months of progress"
rule:     speak to the bridge, never to the pitch — they fear selling
CTA:      /for-founders or /how-to-start

Cross-cutting rule for all four:
Every piece of content must answer: what does this person give, and what do they get? NESEN is an exchange network — content that only describes receiving will attract takers, not contributors.
