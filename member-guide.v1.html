What Is NESEN?

NESEN — New England Science and Entrepreneurship Network — is a structured referral, relationship-building, and pitch network for entrepreneurs, startup founders, executives, and scientists with entrepreneurial intentions.

At NESEN, the principle is simple: your network is your net worth — and it only grows when you invest in others first.

Meetings are weekly, free, in the evening, structured around referral exchange, relationship building, and pitching.

Who NESEN Is For

NESEN serves four distinct groups, each with a different reason to be in the room.

Entrepreneurs

Active business builders who grow through trusted relationships rather than cold outreach. Your classification is yours exclusively — no one in the room competes for your clients.

Startup Founders

From first conviction to first customers. Test your idea against real entrepreneurs, build the network your company will need, and turn a concept into a credible venture.

Executives

Early visibility into innovations before they reach your competitor's desk. A platform to validate internal ideas and build a network independent of any org chart.

Scientists & Researchers

People with deep domain expertise who see commercial potential in their work. NESEN bridges the gap between lab and market — find co-founders, investors, and early customers.

Entrepreneurs

  • Referral exchange — every member is incentivized to send you business. Warm introductions convert faster and stay longer than any cold lead.
  • Peer accountability — a weekly cadence with people who know your business and notice when you go quiet.
  • Complementary network — your classification is yours exclusively. No one in the room competes for your clients.

Startup Founders

  • Path validation — test your idea against real entrepreneurs before committing. NESEN's pitch segment is purpose-built for this.
  • Network building — the relationships your company will need are already forming while you are still building.
  • Case building — weekly exposure to how businesses operate transforms a concept into a credible, presentable venture.

Executives

  • Access to innovation — early exposure to startups and emerging technologies before they reach your competitor's desk.
  • Validation platform — bring internal problems into a room of entrepreneurs. Get real-world feedback you cannot get from colleagues.
  • Personal positioning — build a network independent of your current employer, on your own terms.

Scientists and Researchers with Entrepreneurial Intentions

  • Commercialization guidance — access to entrepreneurs and operators who have taken technology from concept to market.
  • Co-founder discovery — find business-minded partners with the skills that complement your technical depth: sales, operations, fundraising, go-to-market.
  • Credibility building — weekly exposure to how ventures are structured, funded, and sold accelerates the transition from researcher to founder.
  • Network before you need it — the investors, advisors, and early customers your future company will require are in this room before your company exists.

Free Participation

No membership fees. The investment is your time and contribution — showing up, bringing referrals, doing one-to-ones, giving before you expect to receive.

A seat in NESEN is not a passive credential. It is an active relationship with people counting on you to show up and contribute.

Freedom to Build Your Own Success

NESEN creates opportunities. Members remain completely free to decide:

  • how to structure their businesses
  • how to monetize their expertise
  • how to price their services or products
  • which partnerships to pursue
  • which investments to make

NESEN does not participate in, claim ownership of, or receive commissions from transactions conducted between members unless explicitly agreed in a separate arrangement. The value created through introductions belongs to the members who build those relationships.

Our mission is to create opportunity — not control it.

The Weekly Meeting — 90 Minutes

Meetings are held every week at 6:30 PM, 90 minutes, hard stop.

1

Open Networking

Informal, before the gavel. Welcome visitors, schedule one-to-ones for the week.

2

Chapter Updates

Referrals passed, closed business, visitor introductions, open classifications. Transparent numbers make the value visible.

3

Education Moment Optional

One member, 3–5 minutes, on a topic relevant to the group. Every member can suggest an extended educational session for the community calendar.

4

Member Presentations

Each member gives a 60-second update — not a sales pitch, a briefing for referral partners. Three questions: what are you focused on right now, what does a good referral look like today, what should members listen for in conversation. Passing is not permitted.

5

Pitch Segment

One member per meeting delivers a structured pitch for two purposes: feedback from informed peers and activation of the room's network toward your specific asks. Format: 10–12 minute pitch, 3–5 minute response where every member contributes one observation and one introduction or referral. Each member pitches roughly once every 6–12 months. The community also organizes separate events for deeper feedback from the expert community.

6

Referrals and Testimonials

Members pass warm referrals or give public testimonials. A referral is a named person expecting your call, with context. Not a business card. Not "I'll mention you."

One-to-One Meetings

The weekly meeting is 90 minutes. That is not enough to build referral-grade trust. One-to-ones — 45–60 minute conversations between two members outside of meetings — are where the value compounds.

Target: two one-to-ones per month. This is where real relationships form and referrals become a habit rather than an event.

How to Join

1

Visit as a Guest

Attend as a guest — no application needed. Come with a one-minute description of what you do and what introduction would help you most. Bring a member recommendation if you have one — it accelerates the process significantly.

2

Secure Two Recommendations

Before applying, secure two recommendations from existing NESEN members. Recommendations are how the group vouches for you. Members who arrive with two recommendations are almost always approved; those who don't are asked to visit more meetings and build those relationships first.

3

Apply at nesen.org

Submit your application at nesen.org/apply. The process is straightforward once you have visited and have your recommendations in place.

Attendance

Attend the majority of weekly meetings. If you cannot attend, send a substitute — someone not already a member, briefed with your presentation and any referrals you are passing. A covered absence does not count against you.

2 unrepresented absences per quarter is the limit. Exceed it and your classification is at risk.

Rules and Approaches

For Entrepreneurs

  • Your classification is a privilege, not a guarantee
  • Follow up on referrals within 48 hours
  • Report outcomes back to the member who referred you
  • The room sends more business in direct proportion to how well you handle what it has already sent

For Startup Founders

  • Come to learn, not to impress
  • Use the pitch segment to expose weaknesses, not rehearse a polished story
  • Ask for specific introductions rather than general encouragement
  • Treat honest feedback as the resource it is

For Executives

  • Represent your genuine interests, not just your employer's
  • If you're here to scout innovation, say so
  • If you're exploring a personal venture, say so
  • Members who are honest about what they need get the room's full attention

For Scientists & Researchers

  • Bring your real questions, not your polished abstract
  • NESEN is not a conference — you will not be judged on credentials
  • Describe clearly what you are trying to build and what you need
  • The group will help you find commercial language — but you have to start the conversation

Across all four groups:

  • Deliver what you promised at the standard you described
  • Be truthful about your capabilities before taking a referral, not after
  • Follow up on every introduction you receive — the member who made it is watching
  • Stay constructive; the group's energy is a shared resource
  • Where your profession has its own ethical code, it takes precedence

A member who burns a contact referred to them does not just lose that relationship — they damage the credibility of every member who recommended them.

What Success Looks Like

Entrepreneurs

A steady flow of warm referrals that replaces cold outreach entirely. It comes from members who know your business well enough to spot an opportunity in a conversation you were never part of. The return is not immediate — it compounds.

Startup Founders

Arriving with an idea or early traction and leaving — months later — with a validated concept, a network of operators and investors who know your name, and the momentum to keep building. The founders who move fastest show up with specific questions and leave with specific answers and introductions.

Executives

Becoming the person in your organization who sees what is coming before everyone else — and who has the external relationships to act on it. The executives who get the most from NESEN treat each pitch segment as a scouting session and each one-to-one as a relationship worth building on its own terms.

Scientists & Researchers

Closing the distance between what you know and what you can build — a co-founder, a first customer conversation, an investor introduction, or a clear answer to: is this worth pursuing? The scientists who move fastest show up consistently and let the room's collective experience compress years of trial and error into months of focused progress.

Across all four groups, one behavior defines the members who generate the most value: they give without keeping score. They make introductions, pass referrals, and offer feedback without calculating immediate return. The return comes from the group over time — and it is almost always larger than what any individual transaction could have produced.
